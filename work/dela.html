01 / The mandate

Two strangers. One deal. No reason to trust each other.

Peer-to-peer marketplaces run on uncertainty. Sellers worry a buyer will vanish after agreeing. Buyers worry the item will never arrive, or won't match the listing.

Dela set out to make these deals safer and less stressful for Australian users. One product had to serve two people with opposite fears, inside the same transaction.

The stakes

If either side felt unsure at any step, the deal would be abandoned, and the platform would lose both users.

  1. Building trust without face-to-face contact
  2. Preventing ghosting partway through a deal
  3. Making every step clear for buyer and seller
02 / The turning point

The obvious answer was another listings site. The useful answer was a protected deal.

I researched peer-to-peer marketplaces, escrow-style payment models and user complaints about scams and failed deals. The pattern was clear: people could already find things to buy. What broke was finishing the deal safely.

So the product shifted from browse-and-message to a structured transaction. The seller initiates, the buyer confirms, and the platform holds payment until delivery is tracked.

The trade-off

Trust takes a few extra steps

Confirmations add friction. I kept that friction only at the moments where it builds trust, and removed it everywhere else.

The design decision

Make trust visible. Let the transaction carry the reassurance, not the copy.

03 / Key decisions

What shaped the interface.

Decision 01

Show the deal state

Every transaction carries a clear status: requested, confirmed, paid, shipped, delivered. Both sides always know what happens next.

Decision 02

Give buyers the final yes

Sellers start the request, but nothing moves until the buyer accepts or declines. Buyers stay in control without slowing sellers down.

Decision 03

Plain language over finance terms

“Your payment is held safely until delivery” replaced escrow jargon. Clear calls to action and status labels reduce decision fatigue.
